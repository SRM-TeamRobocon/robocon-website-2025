ABOUT SRM TEAM ROBOCON
SRM Team Robocon operates through four specialised domains:

1. SAMBED(Structural Architecture and Mechanical Body Engineering Division)
SAMBED follows a systematic approach, progressing from problem analysis through design and fabrication to final testing and validation. With a strong commitment to precision and performance, the division transforms concepts into reliable mechanical systems through dedication, innovation and engineering excellence.


2. SPACED (Systems Programming Analysis and Computation Engineering Division)
SPACED focuses on embedded systems programming across a diverse range of microcontrollers and single-board computers, including Arduino, STM32, ESP32, Raspberry Pi and NVIDIA Jetson. The division also develops intelligent algorithms for localisation, mapping, path planning, PID control, sensor integration, actuator control and robotic drivers. Web Development (WebDev) forms an integral part of the Coding team and is responsible for designing, developing and maintaining the official website. Through the team’s online presence, they ensure that our vision, achievements and technical progress are effectively shared with a global audience.


3. SIESED (System Integration and Electronics Subsystem Engineering Division)
SIESED bridges the mechanical (SAMBED) and coding (SPACED) domains by integrating contributions from both to develop the electronic systems that power and support our robots. The division specialises in embedded systems design, PCB design, circuit and signal analysis and rapid prototyping, delivering precise, reliable and efficient solutions through focused engineering and continuous innovation.
4. MCSOCD (Media Creation and Social Outreach Creatives Division)
MCSOCD serves as the face and voice of SRM Team Robocon, creating compelling narratives, engaging visuals and impactful outreach. The division, encompassing content writing, graphic design, video editing, public relations, sponsorship and event management, works tirelessly to ensure that our journey is visible, engaging, and memorable. We connect with industry partners, engage with the community and continue to showcase the spirit of reimagining robotics through impactful storytelling, seamless event execution, and consistent creative excellence.

Paper Shredder Hackathon
On 15 September, 2025 team members Agamjot Kaur Choudhary, Mohammed Sadriwala, Nithya Guru K, Siddhant Jain, and Siddhartha Sawant participated in the Smart Paper Shredder Hackathon conducted by the Department of Mechanical Engineering, SRMIST, KTR. The team constructed a portable, energy efficient, low noise smart paper shredder using refurbished/recycled material with integrated control and data monitoring systems. It can shred A4 size paper, detect jams and send an alert when the bin is full. It is powered by solar and hybrid power energy. The paper shredded had shaving blades mounted on four 5mm shafts, supported by custom 3D-printed blade holders. A gear mechanism at the end of every shaft interconnects shafts and transfers motion from the motor. The gear system was powered by a 480rpm, 7.89kg motor to ensure efficient rotation of blades during shredding. IR sensors are integrated to enable automation as they detect the presence at paper input slots. A load sensor is also installed in the bin to monitor weight of shredded paper and relay a signal when it's full. A Phaeton Load Cell Sensor-616BA 1KG aluminum alloy is used for measuring the weight of the bin.

Mechanical Prototyping & Fabrication 
Throughout December, extensive mechanism prototyping and testing was carried out, primarily focusing on KFS picking mechanisms and robot fabrication.
Multiple approaches for KFS picking were tested. One prototype involved servo-motor-based gripping, where a servo motor was mounted on one side of the jumbo gripper to grip the KFS box. Another approach used hook-like plates attached to the gripper, designed to slide under the KFS box and lift it. While this mechanism relied on mechanical limits defined by the KFS box on one side and a channel on the other, it required extremely high precision in depth and distance during scooping, which proved to be a limitation.
Further testing included a prototype using two pistons with sponge-lined gripper surfaces. This design faced issues as the sponges caused the KFS box to slip during lifting. Another variant used a single piston with linkage-based gripping, which was also evaluated.
After multiple iterations, the finalized KFS picking mechanism was selected, incorporating a servo motor on R1. During testing with a magnetic encoder and PID control, chain skipping issues were observed. It was concluded that this could be mitigated through proper lubrication and changing sprocket materials.
A conveyor belt mechanism for R1 was also tested for transporting KFS boxes to onboard storage. Initial testing revealed shaft bending at the return point of the conveyor due to excessive pressure from long, sparsely supported channels. It was observed that shortening the mechanism could resolve this issue. The rubber added to the open-loop conveyor belts acted as an effective stopper and frictional surface, preventing the KFS boxes from sliding.
By 19th December, fabrication of R1 was completed with all finalized mechanisms incorporated, making it approximately 90% mechanically complete. Only one R2 mounting mechanism remains to be tested and integrated. Fabrication of R2’s stair climber mechanism progressed steadily and was successfully completed on 27th December.
Further testing revealed a center of mass imbalance in R1, causing one side of the chassis to lift during motion. This issue was resolved by relocating the power window motor to the base of the robot, centralizing the center of mass and aligning it with the geometric center, resulting in smoother and more stable movement. R1 is scheduled for testing with both 3-wheel and 4-wheel drive configurations, while testing of R2’s stair climber and other mechanisms such as KFS and spearhead picking is ongoing.
Members who were not present in the lab focused on CAD development for finalized mechanisms and overall robot assemblies, along with MATLAB and SolidWorks simulations of the bots.
Electronics, Control Systems & PCB Development 
The SIESED domain played a crucial role throughout December, actively participating in mechanism testing by handling voltages, circuit connections, motor control, and embedded systems, in collaboration with SAMBED and SPACED.
Primary focus areas included PCB design, motor control, and sensor selection. PCBs are being designed with integrated voltage sensors to improve monitoring and system reliability.
The finalized servo-based KFS picking mechanism was tested with a magnetic encoder and PID control. PID performance was evaluated using identical tuning constants while varying the output voltage of the buck converter. It was observed that higher voltages caused abrupt jerks and instability, whereas the system performed optimally at 12 V.
Following the completion of most of R1’s mechanical fabrication, the team shifted focus toward complex electrical integration, including motors, sensors, and essential subsystems required for full robot operation.
 Software, Control Tuning & Testing 
During testing of R1’s KFS gripping mechanism, it was identified that software modifications were required for improved speed control. In early tests, large changes in gripper angle resulted in excessive momentum due to the combined weight of the KFS and the mechanism, causing structural wobbling. Code-level constraints and smoother motion profiles are being implemented to address this issue.
Additionally, battery voltage drop over time was observed, which adversely affected positional PID performance. This issue is currently under investigation and is expected to be resolved in subsequent testing cycles.

The final chassis for both robots was rebuilt based on the improvements identified during testing and design iterations. DD Robocon 2026 Competition
The competition commenced on 4 July 2026. In the first league match, SRM Team Robocon faced KJSSE Robocon from K. J. Somaiya School of Engineering. The match ended with both teams scoring 0 points after R2 malfunctioned due to an incorrect LiDAR configuration.
Following the match, SPACED identified and corrected the LiDAR configuration during the practice session. As the practice spearhead used by the team did not match the dimensions of the actual competition spearhead, SAMBED manufactured a new spearhead rack to practice R2’s staff-picking mechanism accurately. These improvements ensured that R2 was fully prepared for the next match.
League and Knockout Matches
For the second league match against Team RoboVyu from AISSMS College of Engineering, MCSOCD analyzed the opponent’s Kung Fu Scroll (KFS) placement and prepared the match strategy. With R1 completing the first row, the team scored 130 points, securing qualification for the knockout stage.
In the knockout match against PICT Robotics, the strategy prepared by MCSOCD helped the team secure a 100-60 victory, advancing SRM Team Robocon to the quarterfinals.
In the quarterfinals, SRM Team Robocon faced LDCE, the eventual champions of DD Robocon 2026. Both teams scored 60 points. According to the tie-break rule, the winner was decided by the time taken to complete the weapon assembly. LDCE completed the task 4 seconds faster, ending SRM Team Robocon’s competition journey.
Strategy and Team Coordination
At the end of each competition day, the team held review meetings to discuss observations, strategies, frequently encountered challenges, and plans for the upcoming matches. MCSOCD actively observed other teams, recording the placement of Kung Fu Scrolls (KFS), identifying rule violations, and noting the time taken by opponents to complete weapon assembly. These observations played an important role in planning match strategies throughout the competition.
